Puppy potty schedule by age

How often a puppy needs to go out, when the next one is likely, and how long they can really hold it. It takes ten seconds, and there is nothing to sign up for.

Age in weeks or months

For example, 10 weeks, or 4 months.

How often should a puppy pee?

Puppies need to go far more often than adult dogs, and the gap grows as the bladder does. As a working rule, offer a break after every wake, every meal, every drink and every play session, and on the clock in between. The table below is the routine the calculator above uses.

AgeOffer a break about every
Up to 8 weeks40 minutes
9 to 12 weeks55 minutes
13 to 16 weeks80 minutes
17 to 24 weeks115 minutes
6 to 12 months160 minutes
Over a year225 minutes

These are routine intervals, not limits. Offering a break sooner is never wrong, and a puppy who has just eaten, drunk, played hard or woken up will usually need one before the clock says so.

How long can a puppy hold their bladder?

Longer than the routine above. That gap is why the answer you find online seems to contradict the schedule you were given: one number is a capacity and the other is a habit. Two rules of thumb are in common use.

  • One hour per month of age. The American Kennel Club's house-training guide gives roughly the same number of hours as the puppy's age in months, up to about nine months to a year.
  • Months plus one. The same idea with an extra hour, used by Preventive Vet among others.

The AKC also notes that puppies cannot really control the bladder until about 16 weeks, so before then these figures describe a hope as much as a capacity.

AgeUsual ceiling, in a calm puppy
8 to 10 weeks1 to 2 hours
3 months3 to 4 hours
4 months4 to 5 hours
6 monthsabout 6 hours
1 year and over6 to 8 hours, though most dogs go out every 4 to 6

The routine above is deliberately shorter, roughly half to two thirds of the ceiling, because the point is to be outside before rather than after. Plan to the routine. Stretch toward the ceiling only when the puppy is staying dry.

What about overnight?

Young puppies wake in the night, and the trip should be as boring as possible: out, toilet, praise quietly, straight back to bed. No play, no fuss, no lights on. Most puppies still need one trip a night until around four months, and many sleep through by six.

When should I start expecting longer gaps?

Only when the puppy is staying dry at the current interval. Then add ten or fifteen minutes at a time, not an hour, and go back a step after an accident. A puppy toilet training schedule should stretch with the puppy rather than against them, and progress is rarely a straight line.

Does this apply to adult dogs?

The calculator covers puppies up to a year. Past that, a healthy adult dog usually needs out every four to six hours during the day, and can manage longer overnight. If a house-trained dog suddenly needs out far more often, that is a vet question rather than a scheduling one.

Is this veterinary advice?

No. This is a rough guide based on age. Bladder capacity, water intake, activity and health all change how often a puppy needs to go.

If your puppy is going far more or far less than this suggests, or straining, or there is blood, talk to your vet.
