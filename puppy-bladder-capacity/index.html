How long can a puppy hold their bladder?

The number most people are given is one hour per month of age. It describes a maximum, and most owners use it as a routine.

A four month old can physically hold it for about four hours. Four hours between trips is still a bad plan. It means four hours is the outer edge, on a quiet day, at home, with nothing exciting happening.

Intervals that work in practice

AgeOffer a break about every
Up to 8 weeks40 minutes
9 to 12 weeks55 minutes
13 to 16 weeks80 minutes
17 to 24 weeks115 minutes
6 to 12 months160 minutes
Over a year225 minutes

These are how often to offer, not how long to make them wait. Offering more often does not slow house training down.

What shortens the interval

  • Water and food. A drink, a meal or a bowl of wet food. Go out within twenty minutes of eating.
  • Sleep and play. Waking up and finishing a play session are both triggers, and most accidents happen in the two minutes after one of them.
  • Excitement and weather. Visitors, deliveries, cold mornings and rain all shorten it.
  • Size. A toy breed has a smaller bladder than a large breed at the same age.
  • Illness. An infection shortens it suddenly. A puppy who was clean and is asking every twenty minutes needs the vet, not a stricter schedule.

Night time

Most puppies need one or two night trips until around sixteen weeks, some for longer. Keep them boring: out, toilet, straight back to bed, dim light, no play. A puppy that learns night time is interesting will ask for it.

The number of trips should fall as they grow. If it has not fallen by five or six months, or if it climbs again, check for an infection before assuming a training problem.

Stretching the interval

Move in small steps and only while it works. Three clean days at eighty minutes, then try ninety. If accidents return, go back a step for a few days.

Two things make it easier: a trip outside immediately after every nap and meal, and the same door and the same spot every time.

When an accident is a symptom

Worth a vet call: straining to urinate, licking themselves more than usual, blood, stronger smelling urine, or frequent small amounts. In a female puppy, accidents returning after weeks of being clean is the usual presentation of a urinary tract infection.

When to stop tracking

After two clear weeks, not one.

General guidance, not veterinary advice. If something worries you about your puppy's health, talk to your vet.
