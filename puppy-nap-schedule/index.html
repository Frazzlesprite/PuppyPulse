Puppy nap schedule by age

A puppy that bites harder every evening is often a puppy that has been awake too long. Owners tend to read the biting as energy and answer it with more play, which makes the evening worse.

Puppies sleep more than they are awake. Growth and learning happen in that sleep, and a puppy short of it is harder to teach, harder to settle and quicker to snap.

How much sleep, by age

AgeSleep in 24 hoursAwake stretch between naps
8 to 10 weeks18 to 20 hours30 to 45 minutes
10 to 12 weeks18 hours45 to 60 minutes
3 to 4 months15 to 18 hours60 to 90 minutes
4 to 6 months14 to 16 hours90 to 120 minutes
6 to 12 months12 to 14 hours2 to 3 hours, usually two naps

Those are typical ranges. Small breeds, singletons and puppies in a growth spurt often need more. A working breed at six months often needs less. Start with the table and let the puppy tell you the rest.

What a day looks like

Most young puppies settle into a loop: awake, toilet, food or play, toilet, sleep. For a ten week old it runs roughly like this.

  • 7:00 awake, straight outside
  • 7:10 food, then a short play or training session
  • 7:45 outside again, then into the bed or crate
  • 8:00 to 10:00 asleep
  • 10:00 awake, straight outside, and the loop starts again

Four or five loops fill a day. The evening needs the shortest awake stretches of all, which is why the six o'clock zoomies are usually a bedtime problem.

The mistake that makes it worse

Keeping a puppy awake to tire them out. An overtired puppy cannot settle, so the plan produces a more wound up puppy and a worse crash afterwards.

If you have tried everything and they are still frantic, a dark room does more than another game.

Signs you missed the window

  • Biting harder and faster than usual
  • Zoomies that do not stop when you redirect
  • Red or wide eyes, ears back
  • Refusing to settle when clearly tired
  • Losing a skill they had an hour ago

Helping them settle

  • The same spot every time, ideally a crate or pen with a cover
  • Dark and quiet, away from the busiest room
  • Something safe to chew
  • No eye contact and no talking once they are down
  • Ten minutes of grumbling that fades is normal; escalating panic is not

Night sleep works differently

Night sleep consolidates later than day sleep. A puppy that naps beautifully at nine weeks may still need one or two night trips. That is bladder capacity, not a training failure.

When to call the vet

Sleep is a useful health signal. Ask your vet if a puppy sleeps far more than the table suggests and is hard to rouse, if they cannot settle at all across several days, or if the change came on suddenly alongside vomiting, diarrhea or refusing food.

General guidance, not veterinary advice. If something worries you about your puppy's health, talk to your vet.
